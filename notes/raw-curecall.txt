01 · The product

What we are building

A doctor in your pocket — that anyone in India can use.

CureCall connects a person who feels unwell to a verified doctor over a phone call or video — in their own language, paid in rupees, with a permanent health record created automatically afterwards. It has two apps that work as one system.

person
The Patient App
For the person who is unwell

Pick your language, tell the app what hurts, get matched to a doctor, pay, talk, and walk away with a prescription and a record you actually understand.

stethoscope
The Doctor App
For the verified doctor

Get verified once, go online, see a queue of patients with an AI‑written summary ready, consult, prescribe in two taps, and ask peers for a second opinion.

child_care Explain like I'm 6

There are two apps . One is for the sick person and one is for the doctor . The sick person taps "I have a fever," and a little robot helper asks a few questions. Then a real doctor picks up, looks at the answers, talks to them, and writes the medicine down. The medicine note is saved forever — like a sticker book of every time you were sick and got better.

Why it matters — the problem we remove

directions_walk No long travel
Villages are far from good doctors. CureCall removes the bus ride.

translate No language wall
Everything works in the patient's own language, not just English.

history_edu No lost paper
The record is digital and permanent, so history is never forgotten.

03 · Architecture of screens

How screens are wired

One app, one "screen" word at a time

Each app holds a single value called screen . Whatever word it holds is the page you see. A button doesn't "open a link" — it just changes that one word , and the matching page appears. This is a simple state machine.

Example — the patient's screen word

// tap "Pay" →
setState({ screen : 'preconsult' })
// the AI‑intake page now shows

Because state is one word, the app can jump anywhere for demos and QA, and deep‑link to any step. No tangled routing.

Two languages, one switch

A second value, uiLang ( hi / en ), flips every label through a tiny helper tr(hindi, english) . Content is authored as pairs, so nothing is hard‑coded to one language.

title: tr( 'बुखार' , 'Fever' )

14 Case Board — peer second opinions
A Quora‑style debate room for hard cases · screen:'board'

A lone doctor in a village is no longer alone. Tricky cases (anonymised) can be posted for the wider verified community to weigh in — strengthening care quality and doctor confidence.

Posting a case

From a finished consult or the "Post a case" button. The patient is anonymised to a code (e.g. P‑2291 , "F · 34") and the doctor attaches the report, vitals, a proposed diagnosis , and a question for peers.

complaint vitals history meds allergy findings proposed Dx

Reading & debating

Filter All / Mine . Open a case to see the full report, then the responses. Each response is typed support or correct , can be upvoted (per‑response support count) and has threaded replies — a real conversation, not just comments.

The interaction model (state)

openCase(id)
Opens a case into the reading view ( boardOpen ).

postCorrect
Adds your top‑level opinion, typed support or correct ( opinionKind ).

supportResponse(rid)
Up/down‑votes a peer's opinion; toggles supportedByMe & the count.

postReply(rid)
Adds a threaded reply under a specific opinion.

postCase
Publishes a new anonymised case (defaults the board filter to "mine").

04 · Shared systems

The single most important rule

The Safety Gate — a traffic light for every patient

Telemedicine's biggest risk is missing an emergency. The Safety Gate scores every intake and shows a colour on both apps. It is computed once and travels with the patient — same colour on the patient's screen and the doctor's queue card.

check GREEN
Safe to consult online
Everyday complaints with no danger signs. Proceed straight to the doctor.
e.g. viral fever, mild rash, cough

priority_high AMBER
Proceed with caution
Could be minor, could need a closer look. Doctor is alerted to watch for escalation.
e.g. contact dermatitis, possible allergy

e911_emergency RED
Emergency — refer out
Danger signs. The doctor's main button becomes Emergency referral ; the patient is urged to seek in‑person care.
e.g. chest pain, possible heart attack

Worked examples — the three seed patients

Sunita Devi · 34
GREEN Fever 2 days + headache · vitals normal (Temp 38.4, SpO₂ 98%) → viral fever, consult online .

Aisha Khan · 28
AMBER Itchy rash 3 days, new detergent · stable vitals → contact dermatitis, watch .

Ramesh Yadav · 52
RED Chest tightness 1 hr, sweating · Pulse 104, BP 150/94 → possible heart attack → emergency referral to cardiology .

child_care Explain like I'm 6

Before the doctor talks to you, the app puts a coloured light on you. Green = "you're okay, let's chat." Yellow = "keep an eye on this." Red = "this could be very serious — go to a real hospital fast." It helps doctors never miss a scary problem.

How it's decided

A mix of rules (red‑flag symptoms, vital thresholds) and the AI intake model. Rules always win toward caution — when unsure, the gate goes more red, never less. The score and its reasons are stored for audit.

One AI service does four jobs across the journey. Its golden rule: AI assists, the doctor decides. Nothing reaches a patient without a verified human's e‑signature.

forum

Turns the patient's tapped/spoken answers into a clean clinical summary and the Safety‑Gate colour. (Patient screen 10 → Gate 11)

graphic_eq

Converts the consult audio (captured with consent) into a transcript in the spoken language. (Record building, step 1)

edit_note

Drafts the structured clinical note for the doctor, and a plain‑language "understand it" version for the patient. (Record building, step 2 → Record tab)

04 · Shared systems

The memory box

The health record & data model

Everything funnels into one permanent, patient‑owned record. It is the shared truth both apps read from, built on India's ABHA identity and the international FHIR R4 standard so it's portable beyond CureCall.

Core entities

Patient
ABHA address, locale, family members, wallet

Doctor
Council reg., specialty, verification ledger, rating

Consult
Patient + doctor + slot + payment + triage colour

Intake
AI‑normalised complaint, duration, severity, flags

Record
Transcript, clinical note, "Samajh", timeline

Prescription
FHIR MedicationRequest , e‑signed, immutable

ConsentLog
Every grant, withdrawal & record view, timestamped

Ownership & access

The patient owns the record . A doctor sees it only while consent is active; every view is logged and shown to the patient. Withdrawal revokes future access immediately.

Immutability

Signed prescriptions and notes are write‑once . Corrections create a new version (v2) that references v1 — history is never silently edited.

Residency

All personal & health data is stored in India , encrypted at rest and in transit.

FHIR store, consent ledger, immutable versions, India residency.

Cross‑cutting
Idempotency keys on payments; webhooks for async (payment, board decision, payout); every health read/write writes to the consent ledger; rate‑limits on OTP & intake.

Third parties
SMS + voice‑OTP gateway, UPI PSP, liveness/face‑match vendor, ABDM/ABHA sandbox, LLM + STT providers (region‑locked).

Today both apps are self‑contained front‑end state machines (no live backend). These contracts define what to wire next.
