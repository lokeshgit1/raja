Executive summary
What we're building, why now, and how it prints money.
S A M J H O A I S E
A home tutor teaches 20–60 kids from her living room and runs everything on a paper register. Vidwan is a free app that
does her whole job — timetable, attendance, fee collection, report cards — automatically. Parents pay a small monthly
amount to get their child's report card, test alerts and attendance on WhatsApp. The tutor earns a cut of that.
That's the whole business.
The opportunity in one paragraph
India has roughly 71 million students in private tuition (NSSO) and a private-tutoring market worth ≈USD 4.1 billion in
2024, growing ~7.5% a year (IMARC). The individual home tutor and small-batch teacher — 2–4 batches, 20–60 students,
₹800–3,000/student/month — sits at the centre of this and is almost entirely unserved by software. The big edtech names
(Teachmint, Classplus) have moved upmarket to coaching-institute ERP and hardware. The home tutor still runs on a diary,
memory, and awkward fee reminders. That is the wedge.

The product
Vidwan is a tutor-first mobile app (Flutter) with a single backend. The tutor is the only logged-in user. The parent is
reached entirely over WhatsApp — no app, no login. The student is a record, not a user. Five boring things done perfectly:
timetable, attendance, fees, tests, and the monthly report card.
How it makes money — the flywheel
1 Tutor uses Vidwan free
She sets up her batches in under 10 minutes and runs her whole tuition from it. Zero cost removes every reason not to adopt.
2 Parents get free basics on WhatsApp
Attendance pings, fee reminders with a UPI link, and receipts — paid for by the platform because the tutor needs parents to
receive these.
3 Parents upgrade to Premium per child
For a small monthly amount (Hypothesis ₹49–99/child) via UPI Autopay, the parent unlocks the monthly report card, per-test
score alerts and full attendance history — the analytics they actually crave.
4 The tutor sells it for us
She earns 30% of every premium parent she converts. The free tutor becomes an unpaid, motivated salesforce — and premium
reports make her look professional. Revenue scales with student count, not tutor count.
The one-line thesis.
Give the tutor a free tool she can't run her business without; charge the parent for the pride and peace-of-mind of seeing their
child's progress; pay the tutor to grow it. Necessity on one side, aspiration on the other, incentive in the middle.
What we are deliberately NOT building in v1
v1.1+ No live/video classes. No student app. No course/content selling. No tutor-discovery marketplace. No parent app.
No tutor web app (mobile only; internal admin is web). Each "no" protects the timeline and the DPDP posture (Section 25).

The problem & gap analysis
A universal, daily, unglamorous pain that nobody has priced correctly.
S A M J H O A I S E
The tutor forgets who paid and who didn't. She feels awkward asking parents for money. Parents have no idea if their kid
actually attended or is improving. Everyone is guessing. Vidwan replaces the guessing with proof.
The tutor's day today
PAIN 1 · FEE LEAKAGE
Money quietly goes missing
Fees tracked in a diary or memory. Some parents "forget",
some pay late, some short-pay. The tutor has no clean
defaulter list and loses real income every month — often 5–
15% of billings.
PAIN 2 · AWKWARD CHASING
Asking for money hurts the relationship
Reminding a parent face-to-face is uncomfortable, so tutors
delay it, which makes leakage worse. There is no polite,
automatic, deniable channel to ask.
PAIN 3 · ZERO PARENT VISIBILITY
Parents fly blind
Parents pay ₹1,000–3,000/month and receive nothing back —
no attendance, no marks, no report. Trust rests entirely on
word of mouth and the child's exam result months later.
PAIN 4 · EVERYTHING IS MANUAL
Admin eats teaching time
Attendance, scheduling, marks, receipts, reminders — all by
hand. The tutor's scarcest resource, time, is spent on
paperwork instead of teaching.
The gap — why this is unserved
The market has bifurcated. Consumer edtech (BYJU'S, Vedantu, Unacademy) sells courses to parents/students and
competes with the tutor. Institute software (Teachmint, Classplus) sells ERP, live-class tooling and course-storefronts to
coaching institutes — priced and designed for organisations with staff, not a solo teacher. Neither serves the offline home
tutor whose product is her physical living-room class.
The gap in one sentence.
Nobody sells the home tutor a simple account-book-plus-parent-window at a price a solo tutor accepts — and nobody
monetises the parent's desire for their child's analytics without first stealing the tutor's students.

Market analysis — TAM / SAM / SOM
Sized top-down from the industry and bottom-up from per-child revenue.
S A M J H O A I S E
TAM is "how big is the whole ocean." SAM is "the part of the ocean our boat can actually reach." SOM is "the fish we
can realistically catch in the first three years." We size all three so nobody over- or under-dreams.
The market, top-down
India's private-tutoring market was ≈USD 4.1B in 2024, forecast to reach ≈USD 7.8B by 2033 at ~7.5% CAGR (IMARC).
Roughly 55% of Indian students take private tuition (Cambridge International); NSSO counted ~71M students in tuition.
Offline/home tutoring still dominates delivery. This is a large, growing, culturally-entrenched, recession-resistant category —
parents cut many things before their child's tuition.
Bottom-up sizing — Vidwan's revenue is per premium child
Vidwan does not tax the whole tuition economy; it earns on premium parent subscriptions. So we size on students whose
parents could pay ₹49–99/month for analytics. Model assumptions are Hypotheses to validate in pilot.

Why per-child beats per-tutor.
A per-tutor SaaS caps at (tutors × one fee). Vidwan's revenue is (premium children × ARPU), and a single tutor brings 20–60

model can reach nine figures.

Positioning
Vidwan's wedge is deliberately boring: attendance, fees, timetable, reports, trust — executed at a price this segment

Onboarding flow
1 Phone OTP sign-in
Enter mobile number → 6-digit OTP. No passwords. Phone number is the tutor identity.
2 Language selection
Hindi / English / Bangla / Tamil / Telugu / Marathi at launch. Sets app UI language and the default language of parent WhatsApp

3 Profile basics
Name, subject(s), city. Used on receipts and report cards so they go out in her name.
4 Create first batch
Batch name (e.g. "Class 8 Maths — Evening"), days of week, start time, monthly fee per student, fee cycle day (default 1st).
5 Bulk-add students
Add student name + parent WhatsApp number. Fast entry: add one-by-one or paste a list. Each student can override the batch
fee (scholarship / sibling discount).
6 Land on Timetable
Setup complete. The week grid now shows her batches. She can mark attendance and run fees immediately.

Field Type Required Rules

Design rule.
Nothing in onboarding blocks on data the tutor may not have to hand. Only name, days, time, fee, and student name + parent
number are mandatory. Everything else is optional and can be filled later — friction is the enemy of a 10-minute setup.

Feature spec — timetable & attendance
The home screen, and the single most-used action in the app.
S A M J H O A I S E
The home screen is a weekly calendar of her classes. She taps today's class, sees the student list already marked
"present", and just taps the few who are absent. Save. If a child is absent, the parent gets a WhatsApp: "Aarav aaj class
mein absent tha."
Timetable (home)
A week grid (Mon–Sun columns, sessions as blocks) generated from each batch's days + time. Today is highlighted.
Tapping a session opens attendance for that session. A tutor can also add a one-off session or cancel a session (holiday) —
cancelled sessions are excluded from the attendance denominator so reports stay fair.
One-tap attendance flow
1 Open today's session
From Timetable, tap the class. Student list loads, all defaulted to Present (the common case).
2 Toggle absentees
Tap only the students who didn't come — they flip to Absent. Optional "Late" state if enabled.
3 Save
One tap commits the session. Attendance counts update instantly for every affected student's monthly figures.
4 Auto absent-alert
For each Absent student, an optional same-day WhatsApp goes to the parent. Free tier — this ping builds trust and is
funded by the platform.
Rules & edge cases
Case Behaviour
Session on a holiday Tutor cancels session → excluded from "classes held" denominator
Student joins mid-month Attendance % counts only sessions on/after join date
Attendance edited later Allowed within the current cycle; report recomputes from live data
Extra / makeup class Tutor adds a one-off session; counts toward held & attended
Bulk mark all absent Single control for a fully-cancelled day before cancellation is set
Why "default present".
In a real tuition, the vast majority attend. Defaulting to Present means the tutor touches only the exceptions — turning
attendance from a 30-tap chore into a 2-tap action. This is the difference between a feature that gets used daily and one that
gets abandoned.

Idempotency All webhooks deduped on Razorpay event id; charges keyed to (student, cycle)
Failed mandate debit Retry window; parent nudged on WhatsApp; grace period before downgrade

Tutor KYC / linked account Required before rev-share can settle; blocks Flow B payout until complete
Ledger separation Payment.flow ∈ {A,B}; never co-mingled in reporting or settlement
