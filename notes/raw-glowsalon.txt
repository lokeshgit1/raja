Section 1 · The big idea

What we are actually building

Explained twice: once like a story, once like a bet.

🧬 The whole app in one breath

On Sunday evening you want a haircut. You open Glow. It says “Sharma Salon — 20 min wait, 2 people ahead.” You tap Hold my place . You keep shopping. Glow buzzes you when you’re next. You walk in and sit down. That’s it.

THE INSIGHT WE ARE BETTING THE VERTICAL ON

In tier 2–4 cities, booking is not the pain.
Waiting is.

Nobody plans a haircut three days out. They walk in, see six heads ahead of them, and leave. So Glow is not a calendar app with salons in it. Glow is a live queue you can join from 400 metres away. The calendar is a feature. The queue is the product.

What is broken today

You can’t see the wait

Six people ahead or none — identical from outside. So Sunday evening is a coin flip and half the city just doesn’t go.

You can’t see the price

No board, no menu. You ask after the cut. Nobody enjoys that conversation.

You can’t see the work

Salon loyalty is really stylist loyalty — but a stylist’s portfolio lives on nobody’s phone.

Chairs sit empty Tue–Thu

Then bleed customers Sat–Sun. Same shop, same week. The owner has no lever to move demand.

The phone is the booking system

A busy stylist answering calls is a stylist not cutting hair.

Nobody comes back on purpose

No record of who came, what they took, when they’re due again.

What Glow does about it

Live wait, not a guess

Every open salon shows minutes-to-chair, computed by Glow Mind and corrected by the shop.

Hold, don’t book

A free soft claim on your place. Buzzes you at T−15. Expires if you ghost.

Prices on the front page

Service menu with real numbers is the reason to open the app even when you’re not going.

The stylist is the star

Every stylist gets a profile with a before/after wall. Portable, consent-tagged, theirs.

Dead hours get filled

Glow Mind spots the empty Tuesday and pushes an offer to nearby regulars.

Wisbox rails underneath

Same location engine, Wiskeys, storefront and vendor account as the rest of Wisbox.

⚠️ Watch out — the whole thing dies if the wait time lies

If a shop stops marking jobs done, the ETA rots within one hour and users never trust it again. Every design decision in this document bends toward keeping the queue honest with as close to zero vendor effort as possible . Read Section 8 before you argue with any of it.

👨‍💻 Navigation rules

Customer app: bottom tab bar, 4 tabs (Nearby / My bookings / Wiskeys / Profile). /hold/:id/live is modal-over-tabs and cannot be dismissed by back — only by Leave queue. Vendor app: bottom tab bar, 4 tabs (Today / Bookings / Stylists / More). Deep links from notifications always resolve to /hold/:id/live or /today . Cold start with an active hold skips home and lands on the live hold screen.

⚠️ Watch out — back button on the OTP screen

Android hardware back on /otp must return to /phone with the number pre-filled, not exit the app. We lost 8% of testers to this in the Servora build. Do not repeat it.

Auth

Three screens, no passwords. The whole flow should be survivable on 2G in under 40 seconds.

🧬 Signing in

Type your phone number. Get a code by SMS. Type it. Done. No password, no email, no ‘create account’.

Screens

Phone number

Country code fixed to +91 (Phase 1)
10-digit input, numeric keypad only
Auto-advance, no Next button
Continue enabled at exactly 10 digits
Fine print: T&C + privacy, tappable

OTP

6 boxes, auto-read on Android
30s resend timer, then Resend as text
Wrong code → shake + inline error
3 fails → 60s lockout
Change number link, always visible

Profile setup

First name only (required)
Service preference: Men / Women / Both
Skip allowed → defaults to Both
No photo upload in Phase 1
Lands on /nearby

The rules

Rule
Behaviour

One account = one phone
No merging, no multiple logins. Vendor and customer roles can share a number — role picker appears at launch if both exist.

Session length
90 days. Silent refresh. We never log a salon owner out mid-Saturday.

Guest browsing
Allowed. Nearby, salon detail, stylist walls and prices are all public. Auth is required only at Hold my place.

Auth wall placement
Triggered by intent, not by launch. This is the single highest-leverage decision on this page.

OTP provider
Wisbox shared SMS gateway. Fallback to WhatsApp OTP if SMS undelivered in 20s.

Vendor auth
Same flow + a one-time shop code issued by the Wisbox onboarding rep.

👨‍💻 State machine

idle → sending → sent → verifying → success | error . Persist sent across app kill for 5 minutes so a user who leaves to read the SMS in their inbox returns to a live OTP screen, not a fresh phone screen. Store the token in Keychain / Keystore, never AsyncStorage.

⚠️ Watch out — the tier-3 SMS reality

Delivery is unreliable and often 40–90 seconds slow. Do not start the resend timer at request time — start it at gateway-accepted callback. And never block the UI on it: let the user paste the code the moment it lands, even at second 3.

Section 4 · Screens

Onboarding a salon

Ten minutes, phone-only, no paperwork. Onboarding friction is the entire supply-side bottleneck.

🧬 Setting up a shop

A salon owner should be able to put his shop on Glow while a customer is in the chair. Six taps, ten minutes, done. If it takes longer, he stops.

The six steps

1 · Shop basics

Name, photo, phone. Address auto-filled from GPS — he confirms the pin by dragging it, never by typing.

2 · Hours

Open / close, weekly off. Preset chips: 9–9, 10–10, custom. Most shops tap a chip and move on.

3 · Chairs

“How many people can cut hair at once?” A number stepper. That’s the entire capacity model.

4 · Services

Pick from a preset grid of 24 common services. He edits price only. Typing service names is optional.

5 · Stylists

Name + photo per stylist. Minimum 1 (can be himself). Looks can be added later.

6 · Go live

Preview card exactly as customers see it. One button: Open my shop.

Preset service grid — Phase 1

Haircut — men Haircut — women Shave Beard trim Hair wash Blow dry Hair straightening Hair colour Facial Clean-up Bleach Threading Waxing Manicure Pedicure Head massage Body massage Bridal package Hair spa Kids haircut Nail art D-Tan Party makeup Foot spa

👨‍💻 Defaults do the work

Every service preset ships with a default duration (haircut-men 25 min, facial 45, bridal 180) and a suggested price band derived from the city tier. The owner overrides price; he almost never overrides duration — and duration is what feeds Glow Mind. Store duration_source: 'default' | 'vendor' | 'learned' so we know how much to trust it later.

⚠️ Watch out — do not ask for GST, PAN, or bank details in onboarding

Phase 1 takes no money from the vendor, so asking for financial identity here kills 60% of signups for zero benefit. Collect it in Phase 2, at the moment he first earns a payout — when the ask has an obvious answer to “why do you need this?”

👨‍💻 The one interaction that matters

Each row has exactly one tap target that advances state: waiting → in_chair → done . Long-press opens the overflow (reassign stylist, no-show, remove). Drag reorders the queue and immediately recomputes every downstream ETA optimistically, then reconciles with the server. If the reconcile disagrees by > 4 minutes, we accept the server and silently re-render — we never show the owner a conflict dialog. He is holding scissors.

What is deliberately NOT on this screen

No revenue charts

Today's total, one number, in the header. Everything else is Phase 2 /insights.

No customer profiles

He knows his customers. He does not need our CRM on a Saturday.

No settings

Buried in More. Nothing on /today should be configurable.

⚠️ Watch out — auto-advance is not optional

Assume the owner forgets to tap Done. If a job exceeds expected_duration + 10 min with no input, Glow Mind auto-marks it done and quietly moves the queue — then shows a single non-blocking undo chip. Waiting for a perfect vendor is how the ETA rots. Design for the vendor you have.

👨‍💻 Rules of the list

Sorted by priority then recency. Hard cap: 4 cards. Beyond that we are shouting and he stops reading. Dismiss hides for 24h except Critical, which returns in 30 min. Every card must name a specific person, number or thing — never “Your queue needs attention.” The card title is a sentence about his shop, not a system status.

⚠️ Watch out — this screen is a badge, and badges are a budget

The tab badge fires a red dot only for Critical. If we badge Medium cards, he learns the dot means nothing in about nine days, and then it means nothing forever — including on the day it matters.

👨‍💻 Status is a state machine, not a field

held → arrived → in_chair → done , with no_show and cancelled as terminal branches from any pre- in_chair state. Backward transitions are allowed only from done to in_chair , only within 5 minutes, only by the shop. Every transition writes an immutable row to booking_events with actor, timestamp and source ( vendor | customer | system ). Glow Mind trains on this table, so a lie here is a lie forever.

⚠️ Watch out — the same human, two phone numbers

People book on their own phone, then their wife’s. Do not silently merge on name similarity. Match on phone only, and surface “Possibly the same customer” as a suggestion the shop confirms. A wrong merge in a small town is a genuinely bad day for someone.

The moat. Build this properly or build something else.

🧬 The stylist wall

Every barber gets a page with photos of haircuts he did — before on the left, after on the right. You pick the person, not just the shop. Because that’s what you were already doing.

The look uploader — vendor view

Step
Rule

1. Capture
Two photos, same session. In-app camera with a ghost overlay of the ‘before’ so the ‘after’ frames identically. This one detail is the difference between a portfolio and a mess.

2. Consent
A single explicit toggle: “Customer said yes to showing this.” Cannot be skipped. Stores who tapped it, when, and the booking ID.

3. Tag
Auto-filled from the booking: service, stylist, date. Editable. Free-text caption optional, 60 chars.

4. Publish
Goes live instantly. No moderation queue in Phase 1 — but see the reporting rule below.

⚠️ Watch out — consent is not a checkbox, it is a liability

These are photographs of identifiable people, often women, in small towns where everyone knows everyone. Non-negotiable rules: (1) no face-forward image publishes without the consent toggle; (2) the customer gets an SMS with a one-tap “Remove my photo” link, no login needed, honoured within 60 seconds, no questions; (3) removal is a hard delete from CDN and origin, not a flag; (4) any reported image is hidden first and reviewed second. Get this wrong once and the vertical is over.

Add flows

Four sheets. Every one of them has to survive being used one-handed, mid-haircut, on a Saturday.

🧬 Adding things

Four things ever get added: a person to the line, a service to the menu, a stylist to the shop, a look to a wall. Each is one sheet. None is a page.

Add walk-in — the 8-second flow

1

Tap +

Floating button, bottom-right of /today. Always reachable with a thumb.

2

Pick service

The shop’s 6 most-used services as big chips. More opens the full list.

3

Name or skip

“Walk-in” is a valid name. Do not force a name out of a busy man.

4

Add

Enters the queue at the back. Toast: Added · Undo.

The other three sheets

Add service

Preset grid first, custom last. Price is the only required field — duration inherits the preset default. Live preview of how it reads on the customer menu.

Add stylist

Photo + name + years. Optional specialities. Creates an empty wall with a prompt to add the first look.

Add look

Opens the two-shot camera. Consent toggle. Cannot publish without it. See page 12.

Shared sheet rules

Rule
Spec

Presentation
Bottom sheet, 24px top radius, drag-to-dismiss, backdrop 40% ink plum.

Height
Content-sized, capped at 85% viewport. Never full-screen — the queue behind must stay visible.

Primary CTA
Pinned to bottom, above the keyboard. Says the verb (Add to queue). Never says Submit or Save.

Dismiss
Swipe down or backdrop tap. If any field is dirty, confirm: Discard this? — but only then.

Success
Sheet closes, toast appears with Undo, list animates the new row in. No success screen, ever.

Failure
Sheet stays open, inline error on the offending field, CTA returns to enabled. Never a modal alert.

👨‍💻 Optimistic by default

Every add writes to local state and renders immediately, then syncs. On failure, the row gets a rose left-border and a Retry chip — it does not vanish. A row disappearing under a vendor’s thumb during a rush is how we lose the shop’s trust in the data, and the data is the product.

⚠️ Watch out — the + button and the queue order

A walk-in always joins the back of the line, even if the owner adds him while a booked customer is en route. If he wants to favour someone, he drags. Do not build cleverness into the insert position — the owner must own that choice visibly, because the customer can see the order on their phone.

The live feed — what makes a user open Glow on a Tuesday

“Imran is free right now”

Your usual stylist, zero wait, within 1 km. Fires max once per week per user.

“You’re due for a cut”

5 weeks since your last one, learned from your own history, sent on your usual day.

“₹60 off, next 2 hours”

A dead-hour offer from a salon you’ve visited. Only from shops you know.

“New looks from Sharma’s”

A stylist you follow posted work. Pure discovery, no transaction.

“Quiet right now”

Your saved salon is unusually empty. The single most-tapped notification in testing.

Section 7 · Systems

The offer loop, settings & toasts

Where the product and the revenue model are the same object.

🧬 The empty-chair loop

Tuesday at 3pm the shop is dead. Glow notices, offers ₹60 off to twelve people who live nearby and are due for a cut, and three of them come. The shop earns money it would not have earned. That is the only thing we ever charge for.

The loop, step by step

01

Predict

Glow Mind flags a window with < 40% predicted fill, 6–24h ahead.

02

Suggest

Vendor gets one card: “Tomorrow 2–5pm looks empty. Offer ₹60 off?” He taps yes.

03

Target

Sent to past customers within 2 km who are due, and nearby users matching the service.

04

Fill

They hold. The window fills. The offer auto-stops the moment fill hits target.

05

Charge

₹25 per filled dead-hour seat. Nothing else, ever.

👨‍💻 Why the money model lives inside the product, not next to it

Commission on every booking is a tax on customers the shop already had — he will notice within a week and tell people to call him directly, and we lose both the fee and the queue data. Commission on incremental business only is defensible in one sentence at the doorstep: “you pay ₹25 only when I bring you someone on a dead Tuesday.” Phase 1 charges nothing at all — the loop ships instrumented but free, so we learn the real fill lift before we price it.

Section 8 · Engineering

Glow Mind — the wait-time engine

One number, computed honestly — or not computed at all.

🧬 The brain

Glow Mind’s only job is to answer one question honestly: how many minutes until I sit in that chair? Everything else serves that number.

How the number is built

wait_minutes =
Σ remaining_time(jobs_in_chair) // what's running now
+ Σ expected_duration(jobs_ahead) // the line
÷ chairs_available
× stylist_factor // if a stylist is chosen
× time_of_day_factor // learned per salon, per weekday
+ arrival_buffer // no-show-adjusted

expected_duration(service, stylist) =
learned_median if n_observations ≥ 12
vendor_duration if set by owner
preset_default otherwise // cold start
The four jobs

1 · Predict the wait

Per salon, per stylist. Refreshed every 60s or on any queue event. This is the product.

2 · Auto-advance

Job past duration + 10 min with no input → close it, move the line, undo chip.

3 · Spot dead hours

6–24h ahead, flag windows under 40% fill. Feeds the offer loop.

4 · Learn the shop

Every completed job updates that stylist’s median. 12 observations to leave the preset.

Cold start — the first two weeks

Signal available
What Glow Mind does
What the user sees

Nothing (day 1)
Preset durations × chairs. Wide error bars.
“~20–35 min” — honestly wide.

≥ 12 jobs / service
Learned median per stylist per service.
“~18 min” — a tilde, never a promise.

Vendor silent > 25 min
Freeze the ETA, mark Wait unknown , stop advertising a number.
“Busy — wait unknown. Call to check.”

👨‍💻 The graceful-degradation rule

Glow Mind must have a mode where it refuses to answer . Below the confidence threshold the correct output is wait_unknown — not a guess rendered as a precise pill. A salon showing “wait unknown” is fixable; one showing a confident wrong number is a user we never see again. Build the refusal path first.

⚠️ Watch out — Phase 1 has no ML in it

Everything above is medians, counters and a decay function. Ship the arithmetic, collect booking_event for six months, then ask whether a model beats the median. The failure mode is not a bad model — it is building one instead of getting 100 salons to tap Done.

Edge cases — none of these are theoretical

Case
Required behaviour

Customer holds, never arrives
At eta_at + 10 min → auto no-show, queue moves. Third no-show in 30 days → holds require a call-back confirm.

Shop closes early with people in the queue
Every held user gets an instant push + WhatsApp, an apology, and 2 nearby green alternatives. Non-negotiable.

Two customers hold the last slot
First write wins on Idempotency-Key. Second sees a graceful “taken 4 seconds ago” and the next available time, pre-selected.

Hard delete, CDN purge, ≤ 60s, no login, no retention appeal.

Vendor marks 20 jobs done at closing
Detect the burst, mark those ETAs untrusted, exclude from learning.

Queue freezes → 25 min later “wait unknown” → users told honestly. Never keep counting down against a dead phone.
