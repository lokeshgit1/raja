Section 1

What is Servora?

🧬

Like I’m 6

Imagine a restaurant kitchen has lots of jars — rice, paneer, oil. If a jar gets empty, the cook can’t make food. If food sits too long, it goes bad and money is thrown away. Servora is a helper on the phone that watches all the jars. It says “this one is nearly empty, buy more” and “use this one today before it spoils” — and it can even buy things by itself. The cook just taps how full each jar looks. That’s it.

The two problems it kills

🔥 Running out
No paneer at dinner rush = lost sales & angry customers.

🍅 Wasting
Cream spoils overnight = money straight in the bin.

Who uses it

🍽️ Restaurants & dhabas

☕ Cafés & bakeries

🚚 Cloud kitchens & tiffin services

Owner = our user. Low-tech, busy, hates typing.

The 5 clever moves (why it works when others failed)

1 Photograph the menu, not type it. Servora Mind reads the dishes and figures out every raw material. Setup goes from weeks to minutes.

2 Tap “how full”, never weigh. Full / Half / Low / Empty. The AI turns that into real quantity and days of cover.

3 Learn the kitchen in 7 days. It watches what sells vs. what empties, then predicts for THIS kitchen. Then autopilot turns on.

4 Traffic lights = days left, not amount. Half a sack of rice is green; half a tray of paneer is red. Same fullness, opposite urgency.

5 Connect menu ↔ stock. “Paneer spoils tomorrow” becomes “push a paneer combo now.” The loop other tools miss.

Connections & live orders

🧬 Like I’m 6: Plug the billing machine into Servora. Then every time a dish is sold, Servora subtracts the ingredients all by itself — you can watch it happen live.

Connections hub (Plugins)

Lists 4 sources: Wisbox (dine-in & takeaway POS), Petpooja (online hub), and under it Zomato & Swiggy (routed through Petpooja). Each: brand mono badge, connected status dot, and a Connect button or on/off toggle. A “Live order feed” entry sits on top.

Rule: connecting Petpooja auto-connects Zomato & Swiggy; disconnecting it removes them too.

Connect flow (per provider)

1 · Authorise
Restaurant ID + phone. Shows what Mind will read (orders, menu, modifiers) & what it never reads (payments, customer data).

2 · Syncing
Spinner & checklist: importing menu, auto-mapping recipes, subscribing to live orders (~2s).

3 · Done
“84 dishes mapped · 312 ingredient links”. Toast confirms; stock now auto-updates.

Live order feed

A “LIVE · auto-deducting stock” header with connected-channel pills, two stats (orders synced / sales captured), and a “Fire sample orders” button that streams demo orders every ~2.6s. Each order card shows the dish, channel, and the exact ingredient amounts deducted (e.g. −250g chicken). Stock drops & the dashboard ring recolours in real time.

👨‍💻 For the developer: auto-deduct walks each sold dish’s recipe, adds a fractional “depletion” per ingredient, and drops one density bucket whenever depletion crosses 1.0. Only fires for connected channels. In production this is driven by a POS webhook, not a timer.

The spoilage→push loop

🧬 Like I’m 6: If the cream might go bad tomorrow, the app says “sell cream dishes today!” You pick which dishes to put on a deal, push it, and the food gets used instead of wasted. This is the money-saving superpower.

Section 6 · Data & logic

How Servora Mind thinks

1 · Density → quantity → days of cover

A tapped fullness (0=Empty…3=Full) maps to a fraction of a typical pack, then to real units & servings. Days of cover = fullness × cover-per-level (cpl) , capped at expiry for perishables.

cover = density > 0 ? round(density × cpl) : 0
if perishable: cover = min(cover, expiryDays)

2 · Status (the traffic light)

if density==0 → red (out)
else if perishable & expiry≤2 & density≥Half → aging (use soon)
else if cover<1.5 → red
else if cover<4 or (perishable & expiry≤2) → yellow
else → green

This is why a Half sack of rice is green but a Half tray of paneer is red — same fullness, the perishable’s short expiry forces urgency.

3 · The donut ring

Three arcs sized by the share of green / yellow / red items. Built from circumference 502.6 with a small gap between segments. Centre shows the “needs you” count.

4 · 7-day calibration

Learns true per-dish use, burn rate by weekday, supplier lead time, spoilage curve. After day 7, generic cpl defaults are replaced by personalised par-levels & autopilot.

5 · Recipes, fills & posts (supporting data)

RECIPES
Dish → channel, price, and ingredient list with grams & a depletion weight. Drives auto-deduct & offer suggestions.

FILL
The 5 onboarding “hero” items the owner taps first (rice, paneer, chicken, oil, milk).

POSTS
Neighbourhood requests & the owner’s own posts with incoming offers.

👨‍💻 For the developer: all of this is pure functions of state — compute(item) returns {density, cover, status}. The UI never stores derived values; it recomputes on every render so one tap ripples everywhere consistently.

What the backend must do

🔐 Auth & accounts
