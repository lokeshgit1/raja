Executive summary
What we are building, why it works, and where the money is.
? I N P L A I N W O R D S
House parties in Bengaluru already happen every weekend. They get
organised in group chats, which means only the same eight people ever hear
about them, and hosts spend the night counting heads instead of enjoying it.
Gharbar is an app where you find real parties near you tonight, see which of
your friends are going, ask to join, and get a pass with the address on it. If
you are the one throwing it, you set the whole thing up in about two minutes
and the app handles the guest list, the address secrecy and the door.
The opportunity, in one paragraph
Bengaluru has roughly 3.5 million residents aged 18–30, a large share of them recent
migrants working in tech and living in shared flats far from family. Their social life is
almost entirely private and unindexed: house parties, villa weekenders, rooftop
birthdays, dinner tables. There is no product for it. Ticketing platforms serve
commercial events with a seller and a buyer. Social networks serve broadcasting.
Group chats serve coordination but cap a party at the hostʼs existing contacts. Nobody
serves the thing itself — a private night at somebodyʼs home that wants a few more of
the right people in it.

The product
A mobile-first social app with two equally weighted sides and four tabs. The attendee
opens it to find tonight. The host opens it to build a night and run the door. There is no
separate host app, no web dashboard, no ticketing checkout. Six things done properly:
discover, invite, join, host, chat, remember.
Why it compounds
1 A host publishes a night to get more of the right people in
Her existing group chat gives her twelve. She wants thirty, and she wants them
vouched for. That is the only reason she leaves the chat.
2 She invites her twelve, who each bring their graph
An invitation is the strongest install trigger in the product: somebody specifically
wants you at their house on Saturday.
3 Their friends now see parties with real social proof
“Four friends and eleven mutuals are going” converts far harder than any description
of the party could.
4 Attendees become hosts, because hosting is now easy
The single most important conversion in the company. Every host manufactures thirty
attendee sessions and a new pocket of graph.
The one-line thesis. Give the host a free tool that gets her the right thirty people
and takes the door off her hands; give the attendee proof that their friends are
already going; let the invitation do the marketing.
What we are deliberately not building in v1
L AT E R No ticket sales or payment rails. No public commercial events. No dating or
matching mechanics. No follower counts. No stories or feed posting. No web app. No
second city. Each “no” protects the launch date and keeps the trust posture in Section
28 defensible.

Four frictions, all of them weekly, none of them priced by anyone.

Market & the Bengaluru wedge
One city, six neighbourhoods, and a density argument.
? I N P L A I N W O R D S
A party app only works if there is a party near you tonight. So we do not
launch in a country — we launch in six neighbourhoods where young people
already live on top of each other, get it dense enough that the app is never
empty, and only then move outward.
Why Bengaluru, and why these six
Bengaluru has the highest concentration in India of the exact person this product
needs: 22–29, employed, living away from family in a shared flat, with disposable
income and a social circle they had to build from scratch. Crucially, the cityʼs social life
is already house-shaped — long commutes, expensive and early-closing bars, and
large rented apartments push the good nights indoors.

Business model & economics
Free in v1, on purpose. Three revenue lines behind it, in order.
? I N P L A I N W O R D S
At first we charge nobody, because charging for a party nobody can find yet
would kill it. We make money later in three ways: taking a small cut when
someone books a villa through us, selling hosts better tools once they throw
parties often, and letting a few good brands sponsor nights. Guests never
pay us.
v1: deliberately free, and deliberately
not a payments company
Contributions (“₹500 towards the bar”) are displayed as information, never
collected by Gharbar. Guests settle with the host however they already do. This one
decision keeps us out of payment aggregation, refund liability, GST on ticket sales, and
the legal category of “event organiser” — and it keeps a house party a house party.
The three revenue lines, in build order
1 Spaces commission F I R S T
A host books a villa, rooftop or terrace through Spaces (Section 17); we take
10–12% of the booking from the space owner, not the host. High intent, real
money already moving, and the owner is a business who expects to pay for
demand. (Assumption: ₹14,000 average booking, ~₹1,500 per transaction.)
2 Host tools subscription L AT E R
For the ~5% who host monthly: recurring nights, co-hosts, a guest CRM, a
bigger cap, custom invitation art, post-party photo albums. (Assumption:
₹299/month.) Only ships once hosting is provably habitual — charging
occasional hosts suppresses supply.

3 Sponsored nights L AT E R
A spirits or audio brand stocks a real hostʼs real party in exchange for presence
on that partyʼs page. Hard cap: never more than one sponsored night in a
userʼs Discover per week, always labelled, never in the featured slot.
The rule that protects the product. Every revenue line must be paid by a business
(a space owner, a brand) or by a heavy host who is getting real leverage. The
moment an attendee has to pay to see who is going, the social proof becomes a
paywall and the loop breaks.

2 0 · D E S I G N
Design principles
Six rules. Any of them can be used to reject a design, including one of ours.
? I N P L A I N W O R D S
The app should be dark and quiet so the parties look bright and loud. The
photos do the shouting. The buttons and menus stay out of the way.
01 The UI does not scream. The parties do.
Chrome is dark, quiet and repeatable. Colour and brightness are spent almost
entirely on photography. If a control is competing with an image for attention, the
control is wrong.
02 Emotion first, information second.
Every surface leads with the feeling of the night — image, name, who is going — and
only then the logistics. Never show every attribute you have just because you have it.
03 One question per screen.
Progressive disclosure is not a nicety here, it is the reason hosting works. A screen
that asks two things gets split into two screens.
04 Screens that share an object share it visibly.
If a card becomes a hero, it must travel. Motion carries continuity, not decoration.
The app should feel physically connected, never like a slideshow of unrelated pages.
05 Trust is visible, not implied.
Locks, verification ticks and “unlocks when youʼre in” are shown at the exact moment
a user wonders. Safety that nobody can see does not reassure anybody.
06 Warm, not neon.
This is a home at night: lamps, plum shadow, skin tones. Not a nightclub poster. No
cyan, no cyberpunk, no all-over gradients, no glass for the sake of glass.
